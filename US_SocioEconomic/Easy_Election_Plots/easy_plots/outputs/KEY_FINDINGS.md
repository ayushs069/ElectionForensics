# Key Findings — US 2020 Election: Socioeconomic Drivers of Voter Turnout
## 3,141 US Counties | Presidential Election 2020

---

## What the data tells us

### 1. Income → Turnout (Plot 2)
- **Low-income counties: avg 45.6% turnout**
- **High-income counties: avg 55.2% turnout**
- Correlation r = **+0.36** (significant) — richer counties vote at higher rates
- This supports the "resource model": more money = more time, civic access

### 2. Education → Turnout (Plot 3)
- **Least educated counties: ~44% turnout**
- **Most educated counties: ~57% turnout**
- Correlation r = **+0.40** — strongest driver of turnout
- Education is the #1 predictor of voter participation

### 3. Urban vs Rural Turnout (Plot 4)
- **Rural counties median: ~47%**
- **Urban counties median: ~53%**
- Urban counties vote at consistently higher rates
- Rural counties show more variability (some very high, some very low)

### 4. Income vs Who You Vote For (Plot 5)
- Correlation r = **+0.22** — weak positive link: higher income → slightly more Democratic
- BUT: this is not linear. Very rich rural counties vote Republican; rich urban counties vote Democrat
- The real split is urban vs rural, not rich vs poor

### 5. Inequality (Gini) (Plot 6)
- Higher inequality counties (Gini proxy for poverty/disparity) → **lower turnout** (r = −0.12)
- Higher inequality → slightly more Democratic vote (r = +0.25) — driven by urban diversity
- The most unequal counties are often the most racially diverse urban areas

### 6. Development Ladder (Plot 8)
- As education AND income rise together → turnout rises, Democrat vote rises
- Q1 (least developed): 44% turnout, 33% Dem vote
- Q4 (most developed): 57% turnout, 55% Dem vote

### 7. State-Level Variation (Plot 9)
- Highest turnout states (median county): Maine, Wisconsin, Minnesota
- Lowest turnout states: Texas, Georgia, Mississippi
- State context (ID laws, early voting access, mail-in ballots) matter as much as demographics

## One-Line Takeaways for Presentation

> **"Counties that are richer, more educated, and more urban vote at higher rates — and lean more Democratic."**

> **"Inequality (measured by Gini) slightly suppresses turnout, suggesting economic distress reduces participation."**

> **"Education is the single strongest predictor of both turnout AND partisan lean."**
